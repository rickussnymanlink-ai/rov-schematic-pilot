The Complete WR Class ROV 150HP Power Path
This diagram shows your actual SMD system from Ship's 440V supply all the way to the 112kW HPU motor at 3000V subsea.

The key insight: power enters at low voltage (440V), gets stepped UP by transformers in the HV PDU to 3000–3500V, travels down the umbilical, then gets stepped back DOWN inside the subsea termination box.

HV POWER SYSTEM
Drawing AH2646 · Sheets 2 & 3 — How 440V becomes 3000–3500V and why. The heart of the SMD power system.
1 / 5
STEP 01 OF 05
Why Step Voltage UP?
The umbilical can be over 300m long. At 440V, the cable resistance would cause huge voltage drop and power loss by the time it reaches the seabed.

By stepping up to 3000–3500V, the same power travels on much less current — so the cable can be thinner and losses are minimal. This is the same principle used in power grid transmission lines.
≡ƒÆí P = V ├ù I — same power, higher voltage = lower current = less heat loss in the cable (P_loss = I┬▓ ├ù R)

UMBILICAL ALLOCATION
Drawing AG8200 — Every conductor and fibre in the umbilical cable (AB7224). The most important drawing to know as an ROV pilot.
MODULE 3 — UMBILICAL AG8200
Reading the Umbilical Allocation Drawing
This drawing traces every conductor from the HV PDU terminals, through the Deck Cable → Static JB → Slip Ring → Rotating JB → and into the Umbilical Cable itself.

Read it left to right — each column is a connection point. The conductor label (e.g. U1) stays the same all the way through. The terminal number changes at each junction box.

As an ROV pilot, when you lose ROV pod power, check: Is L1/L2 present at the HV PDU terminals? Is it getting through the slip ring? This drawing tells you where to look.
≡ƒÆí The slip ring (AF6768) is the most common cause of intermittent faults — it's the rotating interface between the static deck cable and the rotating umbilical drum. Wear and contamination here causes signal dropout.
Q CORE POD — SUBSEA
Drawing AH2742 — The ROV's brain subsea. Power distribution, Beckhoff I/O, fibre interface, cameras and sensors.
MODULE 4 — Q CORE POD AH2742
The ROV's Subsea Brain
The Q Core Pod (drawing AH2742, 18 sheets) is where all subsea electrical systems live. The 24V PSU Board (AE4151) is the heart — it takes 18VAC from the subsea transformer and produces every voltage rail the ROV needs.

Beckhoff EtherCAT I/O modules handle all digital and analogue I/O — thrusters, valves, sensors. These communicate back to the topside PLC over the fibre link via the Focal Fibre Interface Rack.

The LIM (Line Isolation Monitor) watches for earth faults on the HV system subsea — if seawater gets into a connector and creates a path to earth, the LIM alarms before it becomes a trip.
≡ƒÆí When you see "SHT.14" on a wire reference, that means the wire continues on Sheet 14 of AH2742. Always check the sheet index on the front sheet to find which page covers which system.

The conventions, notation, and shortcuts that make SMD AutoCAD drawings readable once you know the system.
MODULE 5 — READING CONVENTIONS
The 3 Things That Unlock Every SMD Drawing
1. Zone references — Every component and wire cross-reference uses "SHT.X_YZ" format. When you see "SEE SHT.4" on a wire, flip to Sheet 4 and look for the same wire number continuing.

2. Wire numbers are unique — Wire 208-WE appears on multiple sheets but it's always the same physical wire. Follow the number, not the line.

3. Terminal blocks are your anchors — X7-2, X12-1 etc. are physical terminal blocks in the panel. If you're on the ROV and need to trace a fault, the terminal block is where you put your meter.
≡ƒÆí SMD Cabin Settings drawing (AH2713) is your best friend — it shows all transformer tap settings, contactor ratings, and LIM settings on ONE page. Print it and keep it in the control cabin.
